Magic workshops at community centres and libraries: how to set them up

September 2026 · 3 min read

A magic workshop is one of the children's activities that works best in a council's cultural programme: it needs little space, adapts to almost any room, and children go home with something to show. Here's what to bear in mind when setting one up.

Why a magic workshop fits a library or community centre

At a show, children watch. At a workshop, they learn a trick, practise it and perform it in front of the others. That last step is what families value most: the child who dares to perform in front of the group leaves with a confidence they didn't have when they walked in.

In a library, magic also connects naturally with reading. At the end, you can recommend books on magic and illusion from the library's own collection, so the activity carries on beyond that day.

How to plan it: ages, groups and length

  • Age: from 7 or 8 years old. For younger children, an interactive show works better than a workshop.
  • Group size: 15 to 20 participants, so the magician can give everyone attention.
  • Length: 60 to 90 minutes per session.
  • Materials: provided by the magician, and ideally each participant takes home the trick they've learned.

Three formats, depending on what you're after

One-off workshop

A single session within the month's programme. It's the simplest way to try the activity and see how the public responds.

A series of sessions

Three or four consecutive sessions, for example on Saturday mornings. Each week the children learn a new trick, and the last session can end with a short performance for families.

Show plus workshop

First, a show open to everyone. Then a workshop with limited places for those who want to learn more. That way you reach more people without giving up the small group.

What the space needs

A room with tables and chairs is enough. The workshop doesn't need a stage, special lighting or a sound system. If it's combined with a show, a clear space where the audience can sit facing the performer is all it takes.

When to programme it

Magic workshops fit any time of the school year, but there are dates when they work especially well:

  • The Christmas and Easter school holidays, when families look for activities close to home.
  • World Book and Copyright Day on 23 April, known as Sant Jordi in Catalonia, at libraries.
  • The summer programme, alongside other children's activities.

Thinking about Christmas? For the Christmas holidays, it's best to book the workshop two or three months ahead, so now is a good time.

How it's contracted

For a town council, a workshop is contracted like any other cultural activity: with a fixed quote and the usual paperwork. In our guide for cultural officers we explain which documents are usually requested and when each programme should be closed.

Frequently asked questions

From what age can children take part in a magic workshop?

From 7 or 8 years old. For younger children, an interactive show is a better fit.

How many children can take part in each workshop?

We recommend 15 to 20. If demand is higher, you can run two sessions or combine a show with a workshop.
