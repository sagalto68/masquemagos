How to add more magic to your town's Three Kings parade

September 2026 · 5 min read

The Three Kings parade (Cabalgata de Reyes) is one of the most eagerly awaited events of the year in any town — but it's also one of the most repetitive: floats, sweets, and the same route as always. Adding magic at some point during the day is a simple way to make this year's parade one people remember differently.

Why the parade needs something different each year

Year after year, families in the same town turn out for the same parade, with the same floats and the same route. That's not a bad thing — it's part of the tradition — but it also means it gets harder to surprise people every year. A magic show, placed well within the day's program, adds that element of novelty without touching what's essential to the tradition.

Where magic fits into the program

At the Kings' reception

Before the parade kicks off, there's usually a waiting period in the square while the official reception is set up. This is an ideal moment for a magician to entertain families with close-up magic while they wait — especially the youngest ones, already impatient to see Their Majesties.

Along the route, on one of the floats

Adding a visual magic act to one of the floats, designed to be seen from a distance, adds one more surprise to the parade without altering the rest of the route.

In the post-parade entertainment area

Many towns set up an area after the parade where children hand in their letters or collect a small gift. That waiting period is perfect for a magic show that keeps families entertained.

A tip for organizers: booking early is key. Dates in late December and early January fill up fast in every town, so it's worth locking in the booking 2 to 3 months ahead.

A format that adapts to the budget

You don't need a big budget to add magic to the parade. It can range from a single 30-45 minute performance at one point, to a broader entertainment plan spread across several moments of the day, depending on what the town council has planned for the Christmas and Three Kings festivities.
