How to choose the perfect magician for a kids' birthday party

July 2026 · 6 min read

What to ask before hiring, and why a good magician knows how to adapt to any mix of ages. A practical guide so the magician you choose is the highlight of the party, not an unpleasant surprise.

Age isn't what matters: the ability to adapt is

It's common to think you need to look for "a magician for 6-year-olds" or "a magician for 10-year-olds". In reality, most kids' birthday parties bring together children of very different ages at the same event — it's completely normal for a party to have guests aged 4 to 9 at once, cousins of different ages, school friends and neighbourhood friends all mixed in.

That's why the question that really matters isn't "what kind of magic do you do for that age?", but rather: can you adapt to a group with mixed ages within the same show? A good professional magician reads the room live and engages everyone equally, whatever their age. A magician without real experience is the one who always does the same show, exactly the same, regardless of who's watching — and that's where the difference shows between a party that's a hit and one that falls flat.

The key question to ask before hiring: "How do you adapt if the party has children of very different ages?" If the answer is vague, or they tell you they always do the same show regardless, that's a red flag.

The questions that really matter before hiring

1. How much experience do they specifically have with kids' birthday parties?

Doing close-up magic in a bar isn't the same as holding the attention of 15 children for an hour. Ask directly how many kids' birthday parties they've done, not just whether they "do magic".

2. Can they show video of a real performance, with real children?

A nice-looking website isn't the same as seeing how a group of real kids reacts during the show. Ask to see something filmed at a real party before deciding.

3. What happens if a child doesn't want to participate, or starts crying?

It's more common than you'd think, especially with younger children. A magician with real experience knows how to handle these situations without it showing or breaking the party's rhythm. If the answer makes you feel like they've never thought about this, that's a bad sign.

4. Does the price include all the material and sound equipment?

Always ask this before agreeing on a price, so there are no surprises on the day. At Más que Magos, for example, sound equipment is always included — no need to worry about hiring anything separately.

5. How long does the show last?

The usual and recommended length is 1 hour. A good magician knows how to structure that time so it never drags, whatever the age of the children in front of them.

Why you should be wary of a price that's too low

It's tempting to go for the cheapest option, but in children's magic, price tends to track closely with the real quality of the show. A magician who charges very little is rarely a coincidence: it usually means little experience, little of their own material, or little real commitment to each party.

And with a kids' birthday party there's no room for error: you can't "redo" it if the show doesn't work. The one hour the magician has to connect with the children is the only one they'll get, so it's worth investing in someone you know will do it well.

Signs you've found a good magician

  • They explain how they adapt to a group with mixed ages, without you having to ask first
  • They have reviews from other kids' birthday parties, not just adult events
  • They answer your questions calmly and in detail, including the "what if..." ones
  • They tell you in advance what they bring and what they need from the space (a plug socket, clear floor space, etc.)
