Magic as a team building activity: why it works better than the usual games

August 2026 · 5 min read

How a magic show builds team cohesion in a way that classic team building activities can't quite achieve, and how to fit it into a team building day.

The problem with the usual team building activities

Escape rooms, scavenger hunts, cooking workshops, corporate role-play games... most teams have already been through nearly every classic team building format out there. They work, but it gets harder every time to actually surprise people, and the activity can easily feel like "just another one" instead of a moment people genuinely remember.

The real challenge isn't just entertainment: it's getting the team to interact differently than usual, building real rapport between people who may have worked together for months without ever talking outside a meeting.

Why magic achieves something different

It creates shared amazement. When a group of coworkers experiences a moment together they can't explain, an instant bond forms: everyone looks at each other searching for an explanation, laughs together, talks about what they just saw. That shared "I can't believe it" moment is hard to replicate with other activities.

It breaks down hierarchy, even briefly. During a close-up magic performance, the CEO and the intern who just started are on equal footing: both are equally amazed spectators. That momentary equality helps team relationships feel a bit more human and less hierarchical for the rest of the day.

It doesn't require physical effort or awkward exposure. Unlike many team building activities that force people to "perform" or put themselves on the spot in front of others (which makes plenty of people uncomfortable), magic is something you simply receive: there's nothing strange required to enjoy it, which makes it far more inclusive for teams with very different personalities.

Worth knowing: unlike other activities, magic works equally well for teams that already know each other very well and for teams that just merged, or that work remotely and rarely see each other in person.

How to fit it into a team building day

  1. As an icebreaker at the start of the day, with close-up magic while the team arrives and grabs coffee, to build a good atmosphere before the rest of the programme kicks off.
  2. As the central activity, with a magic workshop where the team itself learns a few simple tricks and practises in pairs — a different way of working on communication and trust.
  3. As a closing moment, with a stage show that brings the whole team together to end the day with a shared experience, before dinner or the closing cocktail.
