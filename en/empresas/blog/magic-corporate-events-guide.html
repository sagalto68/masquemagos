Magic for corporate events: the complete guide

September 2026 · 6 min read

If you're looking for entertainment for a corporate event, you've probably already seen the usual options: a DJ, a photo booth, catering with a show-cooking station... Magic is an option many companies haven't tried yet, and that's exactly why it's the one people remember most. This guide walks through how it fits each type of event.

Why magic works in a corporate setting

A corporate event has a challenge other celebrations don't: the audience doesn't always want to be there by choice. There are colleagues who don't know each other, hierarchies that create distance, and a general sense of "this is still work, even with a drink in hand." Magic breaks through that barrier in a way no other entertainment does: it's impossible not to pay attention when something happens right in front of you, and for those minutes hierarchy disappears. Everyone reacts the same way to something they can't explain.

What kind of corporate event works with magic

Corporate dinners and cocktails

The most common format. Close-up magic moves between tables or works the welcome cocktail, filling those dead spots every dinner has. It's the easiest option to add because it doesn't require changing anything already planned.

Incentive trips

On an incentive trip, every night or dinner is a chance to surprise the team in a different way. Magic fits especially well as the closing act of an intense day, once the group is relaxed and ready to enjoy themselves.

Team buildings and team days

Beyond magic as a show, it can also be framed as a participatory activity: employees themselves learn small magic tricks and practice them with each other. It's a different way to build trust and communication within the team.

Trade fairs, conferences and product launches

At a booth or trade fair, a magician acts as a crowd magnet: catching the attention of passersby and holding it long enough for the sales team to start a conversation. The product or brand can also be woven directly into the magic tricks themselves.

A tip: the sooner you define the type of event and the exact moment you want to bring magic in, the easier it is to adapt the format — close-up, stage, or a mix — to what you actually need.

Close-up magic or stage magic: how to choose

Close-up magic is performed table by table or group by group, with no need to stop the event or ask for silence. It's the most versatile option and the one that best fits cocktails, dinners and trade fairs.

Stage magic is a show aimed at the whole audience at once, designed as the centerpiece or closing moment of the event. It works best when there's a clear space and moment set aside for it, such as before the speeches or at the end of the day.

In many events, combining both formats — close-up during the cocktail, stage as the closing act — covers the whole event best from start to finish.
