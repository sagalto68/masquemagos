Magic at the stand: how to attract visitors at a trade fair

September 2026 · 5 min read

At a trade fair, every stand competes for the same few seconds of attention from a visitor walking briskly past aisles full of brochures. The marketing lead's question isn't what to say, but how to get people to stop and listen.

Why magic outperforms the usual tactics

A promoter repeating "would you like to hear about our product?" produces the opposite of what's intended: people speed up. Magic does the reverse. When someone sees a small crowd forming around something surprising, curiosity wins and they come over on their own.

It's attention earned without interrupting — exactly what a brand needs in an environment as saturated as an exhibition hall.

How it ties into the brand message

A magician experienced in trade fairs doesn't perform generic magic at your stand: the tricks are adapted so the product, logo or campaign message becomes part of the effect itself. A card bearing the brand name appearing inside a sealed object, or the product itself as the centrepiece of the impossible moment.

That way the memory attaches to the feeling of astonishment, not just to a brochure that gets binned on the way out.

How often should the set repeat? At a full-day fair, short ten to fifteen minute sets every hour work better than a single long show: they generate steady attention without wearing out the stand team.

It doesn't replace your sales team — it opens the door

The magician doesn't sell. They create the group of people standing in front of your stand, and that's where the commercial team steps in to talk to visitors already willing to listen, rather than having to approach people hurrying past.

The practical difference is the kind of conversation: one that starts with "good morning, are you interested in…?" is not the same as one that starts after the visitor has just laughed at something they can't explain.

What to ask before hiring

  • Do they have specific trade fair experience? It's a different environment from a stage: there's noise, people come and go, and the act has to run on loop for hours.
  • Can they adapt the tricks to our product? If the answer is generic, the performance will be too.
  • How do they coordinate with the sales team? They need to know when to step back so the team can talk.
